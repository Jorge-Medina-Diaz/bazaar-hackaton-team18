# Glossary — Never Split the Difference

**7-38-55 rule** — words carry 7 % of a message's impact, tone 38 %, body language 55 % (Ch 8)
**Accommodator** — bargaining style that values the relationship and time spent together, and may over-promise (Ch 9)
**Accusation Audit** — listing out loud, in advance, every negative thing the other side may think of you (Ch 3)
**Ackerman model** — offer plan of 65/85/95/100 % of target, decreasing steps, precise final number plus a nonmonetary item (Ch 9)
**Analyst** — methodical, data-driven style; silence means thinking; dislikes surprises (Ch 9)
**Anchor** — the first reference number or emotion that frames the rest of the negotiation (Ch 6)
**Assertive** — style that sees time as money and needs to be heard before listening (Ch 9)
**Behavioral Change Stairway** — active listening → empathy → rapport → influence → behavioral change (Ch 5)
**Black Swan** — unknown unknown that, once uncovered, changes the negotiation (Ch 10)
**Black Swan rule** — treat others the way they need to be treated, not the way you'd like to be (Ch 9)
**Calibrated question** — open "What/How" question that gives the counterpart the illusion of control (Ch 7)
**Chris Discount** — using your own name to humanize yourself and earn a concession (Ch 8)
**Counterfeit yes** — a "yes" said to escape the conversation, not to agree (Ch 4)
**Effective pause** — deliberate silence that lets a mirror or label work (Ch 3)
**Fair** — the most powerful word; used as an accusation it puts the other side on the defensive (Ch 6)
**Forced empathy** — making the counterpart see your side by asking them to solve your problem (Ch 7)
**Illusion of control** — letting them feel in charge while you steer with questions (Ch 7)
**Labeling** — naming their emotion or position: "It seems like…" (Ch 3)
**Late-Night FM DJ voice** — calm, slow, downward-inflecting voice that signals certainty (Ch 2)
**Leverage (positive / negative / normative)** — power from giving what they want / threatening a loss / their own norms (Ch 10)
**Loss aversion** — people take more risk to avoid a loss than to get an equal gain (Ch 6)
**Mirroring** — repeating the last 1–3 words to get them to elaborate (Ch 2)
**Negotiation One Sheet** — prep template: goal, summary, labels, calibrated questions, noncash offers (Appendix)
**No-oriented question** — question designed so that "No" moves you forward (Ch 4)
**Pinocchio effect** — liars use more words, more third person and complex sentences (Ch 8)
**Rule of Three** — getting agreement three times to make it stick (Ch 8)
**Strategic umbrage** — controlled show of offense to reset an outrageous anchor (Ch 9)
**Summary** — paraphrase plus label that earns "That's right" (Ch 5)
**System 1 / System 2** — fast emotional thinking / slow deliberate thinking (Ch 1)
**Tactical empathy** — understanding and voicing their perspective in order to influence it (Ch 1, 3)
**"That's right"** — the breakthrough phrase: they feel understood (Ch 5)
**Three kinds of yes** — counterfeit, confirmation, commitment (Ch 4)
