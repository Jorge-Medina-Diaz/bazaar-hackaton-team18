## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The premise.** People are irrational, emotional System 1 deciders. Negotiation is information-gathering and behavior-influencing, not a logic contest. **Tactical empathy** means understanding the other side's feelings and mindset, and saying that understanding out loud, so you can influence them. Empathy is not agreement.

**Listening tools (ch2–3)**
- **Mirroring**: repeat the last 1–3 words they said, with an upward inflection, then go silent. Use it to buy time, make them elaborate and build rapport. "I'm sorry…" + mirror + silence works as a safe assertive move.
- **Labeling**: "It seems like… / It sounds like… / It looks like…", then silence. Use it to defuse negative emotion and reinforce positive emotion. Never start with "I".
- **Accusation Audit**: before they can, list every negative thing they might think about you ("You're going to think I'm being unreasonable…"). Saying the negatives out loud takes the sting out.
- **Voices**: the *Late-Night FM DJ voice* (calm, slow, downward) for points that aren't up for discussion; the *positive/playful voice* by default. Avoid the direct/assertive voice.
- **Effective pauses**: silence after a mirror or label is part of the technique.

**"No" and "That's right" (ch4–5)**
- **Beware "Yes", master "No"**: "Yes" can be counterfeit, a confirmation or a commitment. "No" makes them feel safe and in control. Ask **No-oriented questions**: "Would it be a bad idea to…?", "Is now a bad time…?", "Have you given up on…?" (the last one is the 9-word re-engagement email).
- **"That's right" > "You're right"**: a **Summary** (paraphrase + label of their world view) that earns "That's right" is the breakthrough. "You're right" means they want you to stop talking.

**Bending reality (ch6)**
- **Never split the difference**: meeting in the middle ("wearing one black shoe and one brown shoe") is a bad deal for both. **No deal is better than a bad deal.**
- **Deadlines cut both ways**: when it's over for one side, it's over for the other. Deadlines are usually self-imposed and flexible. Revealing yours lowers the risk of impasse.
- **"Fair"** is the most powerful word. Never use it defensively ("we just want what's fair"). Use it positively: "I want you to feel I'm treating you fairly. Stop me if I'm not."
- **Anchor their emotions**: run an Accusation Audit first, then frame the offer as a **loss** (loss aversion beats gain). Let them anchor first on price when you can. Use **ranges** (a high range anchors without committing), **odd/precise numbers**, **pivots to nonmonetary terms**, and surprise **gifts**.

**Calibrated questions (ch7)**: "What/How" open questions with no yes/no and no "why". They create the *illusion of control* and **forced empathy**: "How am I supposed to do that?", "What about this works for you?", "How can we solve this?" Suspend unbelief and keep your emotions in check.

**Guarantee execution (ch8)**: "Yes" is nothing without "how". Find the **deal-killers behind the table** ("How does this affect your team?"). Use the **Rule of Three** (get agreement three times in three forms). Watch for the **7-38-55 rule** (words, tone, body), the **Pinocchio effect** (liars use more words and third person) and the **pronoun tell** ("I/me" = less power, "we/they" = more). **Chris Discount**: humanize yourself with your name.

**Bargain hard (ch9)**
- **Know the three styles**: **Analyst** (methodical, silence = thinking, hates surprises), **Accommodator** (relationship first, talks a lot, may over-promise), **Assertive** (time = money, needs to be heard first). Use the **Black Swan rule**: treat others the way *they* need to be treated, not the golden rule.
- **Taking a punch**: deflect an extreme anchor with "No", a calibrated question, or a pivot to terms, and don't counter right away. Use **strategic umbrage** and "I'm sorry, that just doesn't work for me."
- **Ackerman model**: (1) set a target. (2) Open at **65 %** of target. (3) Raise to **85 %, 95 %, 100 %**, in decreasing increments. (4) Use empathy and "No" between steps so they counter first. (5) The final number is **precise and non-round**. (6) Add a **nonmonetary item** to the final offer to show you're at your limit.

**Black Swans (ch10)**: unknown unknowns that change everything. Find **leverage** (positive, negative, normative: use their own rules), learn their **worldview / religion**, know that they're **not crazy**: they're ill-informed, constrained, or have other interests. Similarities and **"Face Time"** reveal them. Learn the most at the start and the end.

**Negotiation One Sheet (appendix)**: goal (optimistic, specific), summary ("that's right"), labels/Accusation Audit, calibrated questions, noncash offers.
