# Patterns — Never Split the Difference

## Mirroring
**When to use**: you need more information, time, or rapport.
**How**: repeat the last 1–3 words with an upward inflection, then stay silent. Add "I'm sorry…" before it when you disagree.
**Trade-offs**: it feels strange the first time and can come across as mechanical if you overuse it.

## Labeling
**When to use**: emotions (theirs) are blocking progress, or you want to reinforce a positive one.
**How**: "It seems / sounds / looks like…" + the emotion or dynamic, then pause. Never say "I'm hearing…".
**Trade-offs**: a wrong label is cheap ("I didn't say that") and still gets them talking.

## Accusation Audit
**When to use**: before delivering bad news or a tough offer.
**How**: list every negative they might think about you and say it first, bluntly.
**Trade-offs**: if you overdo it, it sounds rehearsed. Stick to the real fears.

## No-oriented questions
**When to use**: you want engagement without making them feel trapped.
**How**: phrase the question so that "No" is the answer you want ("Is it a bad idea to…?").
**Trade-offs**: works poorly with people who answer literally.

## Summary → "That's right"
**When to use**: before asking for a change of behavior.
**How**: paraphrase their position + label their emotions, until they say "That's right".
**Trade-offs**: "You're right" is a false positive.

## Calibrated questions
**When to use**: they made a demand you can't meet, or you want them to solve your problem.
**How**: start with What or How. Examples: "How am I supposed to do that?", "What's the biggest challenge…?"
**Trade-offs**: repeated too often they feel interrogated. Mix in labels.

## Anchoring emotions + loss framing
**When to use**: before any number.
**How**: Accusation Audit, then frame what they lose by not accepting. Let them anchor first if you're unsure of the market.
**Trade-offs**: a wrong extreme anchor can end the talk with a sophisticated counterpart.

## Ranges, odd numbers, nonmonetary pivots
**When to use**: setting expectations without committing.
**How**: quote a range that starts at your target, use precise numbers, and offer things that are cheap to you and valuable to them.
**Trade-offs**: a range means they'll pick the bottom, so the bottom must be your target.

## Ackerman bargaining
**When to use**: pure price haggling.
**How**: target → 65 / 85 / 95 / 100 % in decreasing steps, with empathy and calibrated questions in between. The final number is precise, plus a nonmonetary item.
**Trade-offs**: rigid against someone who knows the system. Requires patience.

## Taking a punch
**When to use**: an extreme anchor or an aggressive opener.
**How**: "No" / "I'm sorry, that doesn't work for me", a calibrated question, or a pivot to terms. Don't make a counter-number yet.
**Trade-offs**: strategic umbrage risks escalation. Keep the FM DJ voice.

## Rule of Three
**When to use**: confirming that a "yes" is real.
**How**: get agreement three times: the yes, a summary/label they confirm, and a "how" they describe.
**Trade-offs**: it takes time, so spread it across the conversation.

## Black Swan hunting
**When to use**: always, especially when the other side looks "crazy".
**How**: ask open questions, listen for worldview and constraints, use face time, look for leverage (positive, negative, normative).
**Trade-offs**: needs listening time, which pays off at the start and the end.
