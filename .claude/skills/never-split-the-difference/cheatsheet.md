# Cheatsheet — Never Split the Difference

## Decision rules
- **They make an extreme anchor** → don't counter yet. Say "How am I supposed to do that?" or "No", or pivot to terms. Countering right away validates their anchor.
- **They propose meeting in the middle** → refuse. Splitting is a bad deal for both. Make your next (smaller) step instead.
- **They said "yes" too fast** → it's counterfeit until you hear "how". Ask "What's the biggest challenge you face in making this happen?"
- **You need agreement** → ask a No-oriented question ("Would it be ridiculous to…?"). "No" feels safe to them.
- **Emotion is running high** → label it ("It seems like…") and pause. Don't argue facts.
- **You're about to make an offer they'll hate** → run an Accusation Audit first and frame the offer as a loss avoided.
- **They went silent / ghosted** → send "Have you given up on X?"
- **They said "You're right"** → you haven't connected. Summarize their view until they say "That's right".
- **You want them to solve your problem** → ask a calibrated How/What question, never "Why".
- **Deadline is near** → it binds both sides. Don't rush concessions. The side that hides its deadline negotiates against itself.

## Ackerman (bargaining offers)
| Step | % of target | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 65 % | extreme anchor, wrapped in empathy |
| 2 | 85 % | only after they counter or you ask a calibrated question |
| 3 | 95 % | smaller step, signals the floor is near |
| 4 | 100 % | precise non-round number + a nonmonetary item |
Decreasing increments tell them you're squeezed dry.

## Thresholds & defaults
- Mirror = last **1–3** words. Then silence.
- Label, then pause for **at least 4 seconds**.
- **Rule of Three**: get the same commitment three times, in different forms.
- **7-38-55**: words 7 %, tone 38 %, body 55 %. Trust the tone over the words.
- Ranges: anchor with a range whose bottom is your real target.

## Tells & smells
| Tell | Likely meaning |
|---|---|
| Lots of words, third person, complex sentences | **Pinocchio effect**: possible lie |
| Uses "we/they" a lot, avoids "I" | Decision-maker who keeps options open |
| Specific threats (what/who/when/how) | Their real deadline is near |
| "Fair" used against you | Defend with "Let's go back to where I started treating you unfairly…" |
| Silence after your offer | Analyst thinking, not refusing |
| Rapid-fire talking, wants to be heard first | Assertive: let them talk, then summarize |

## Counterpart styles
| Style | Wants | Silence means | Do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Analyst | data, no surprises | thinking | give data and time; don't push |
| Accommodator | relationship | anger | get the "how"; they may over-promise |
| Assertive | to be heard, speed | chance to talk | summarize, let them feel heard first |
