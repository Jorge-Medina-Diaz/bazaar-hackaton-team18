# Chapter 2: Be a Mirror

## Core Idea
Negotiation is discovery, not a battle of arguments. Quiet the voices in your head, focus entirely on the other person, slow down, and use your voice and Mirroring to make them feel safe enough to keep talking. The more they talk, the more information (and the more "Black Swan" surprises) you reveal.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Mirroring (isopraxism)**: Repeat the last three words, or the critical one to three words, of what someone just said.
  - When to use: To keep them talking, draw out elaboration, buy time, build rapport, disagree without being disagreeable.
  - How: (1) Listen for the key words. (2) Repeat them, usually as a mildly inquisitive question. (3) Stay silent and let it work. (4) Expect them to rephrase and elaborate.
- **Late-Night FM DJ Voice**: Deep, slow, soft, downward-inflecting, calm.
  - When to use: Selectively, to make a point, de-escalate, or deliver a non-negotiable item without triggering defensiveness.
  - How: Slow down, lower pitch, inflect downward so statements sound like "I've got it covered."
- **Three voice tones**: Late-Night FM DJ (selective), positive/playful (default), direct/assertive (rarely; it provokes pushback).
- **Four-step confrontation sequence**: (1) DJ voice, (2) start with "I'm sorry...", (3) Mirror, (4) Silence for at least four seconds, then repeat.
- **Hypotheses, not assumptions**: Hold several hypotheses about the situation and counterpart; test and discard them as information arrives.

## Key Concepts
- **Slow. It. Down.**: Hurry makes people feel unheard; slowing the process also calms it.
- **"Schizophrenic" listening state**: Both sides absorbed in their own internal arguments; solved by focusing solely on the other person.
- **Wants vs needs**: Wants are aspirations that are easy to talk about; needs are the survival minimum; build safety to reach needs.
- **Pronoun clue**: The less important someone makes himself (heavy "they" vs "I"), the more important he probably is.
- **Smile**: A smile audible in the voice raises mental agility in both parties.
- **Mirroring principle**: We fear what is different and are drawn to what is similar.
- **Vomiting information**: When a mirror makes a counterpart blurt out damaging details.
- **Team listening**: FBI uses several listeners because good listening is hard.

## Mental Models
- Use the positive/playful voice by default; use the DJ voice when you must hold a line or calm someone.
- Use mirrors with "I'm sorry..." when you cannot ask "What do you mean?" without sounding defensive.
- Use silence after a mirror; the other party fills it.
- Use "Joe's gone. You're talking to me now" style downward statements to make declarations that need no negotiation.
- Treat what you think you know as hypotheses until verified.

## Anti-patterns
- Rushing to a quick solution; being a problem solver instead of a people mover.
- Preparing your next argument while they speak.
- Stepping on your own mirror with a follow-up question (Voss's "Are you Chris Watts?" let the robber dodge).
- Using upward inflection when you want to sound in control.
- Using the assertive voice as a default.
- Believing early intelligence ("they want to surrender") without testing it.

## Worked Example
Bank robbery standoff, Brooklyn 1993. Voss, in DJ voice: "We chased your driver away?" The robber, rattled, reveals the getaway driver and an accomplice. Later "Are you Chris Watts?" lets him say "No"; Voss recovers: "No? You said 'okay.'" and then stays silent. The robber blurts more info, and the second hostage-taker, Bobby, comes on the phone and surrenders.
Office version: Boss: "Let's make two copies of all the paperwork." Employee: "I'm sorry, two copies?" Boss: "One for us and one for the customer." Employee: "Anywhere?" (mirroring "You can store it anywhere") The boss pauses and settles for two digital backups.

## Key Takeaways
- Negotiation is discovery; gather as much information as you can.
- Make the other person your only focus and slow down.
- Smile; use the playful voice by default.
- Mirror the last one to three words, then be silent.
- Treat assumptions as testable hypotheses.
- Mirrors disagree without confrontation and reveal strategy.
