# Chapter 1: The New Rules

## Core Idea
Classic negotiation theory (Getting to Yes, BATNA/ZOPA, rational problem solving) assumes rational actors, but people are driven by emotion. Voss, as FBI lead kidnapping negotiator, built a system on psychology and crisis intervention that beat Harvard-trained negotiators. Negotiation is "communication with results," used for information gathering and behavior influencing, and it applies to every interaction in life. The central tool is Tactical Empathy: listening as a martial art.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Tactical Empathy**: Understanding the feelings and mindset of another in the moment, and what is behind them, so you increase influence in every later moment.
  - When to use: In every negotiation, from the first minute; it is the foundation for all other tools.
  - How: (1) Listen intensely, making the other side the sole focus. (2) Recognize their perspective. (3) Vocalize that recognition. (4) Use the calmer, less defensive state it creates to steer toward agreement.
- **Calibrated Questions (preview)**: Open-ended "How?" / "What?" questions the other side can answer but that have no fixed answer.
  - When to use: To buy time, hand the counterpart the illusion of control, and make them solve your problem.
  - How: Respond to threats, demands and logic traps with "How am I supposed to do that?" repeated in variations, then stay quiet while they negotiate against themselves.
- **System 1 / System 2 influence (Kahneman)**: Affect the fast emotional mind by how you frame and deliver words; the slow rational mind then rationalizes the result.
  - When to use: Whenever a logical case is not landing.
  - How: Change the emotional framing first (tone, question form), then let their logic follow.

## Key Concepts
- **Getting to Yes (Fisher and Ury)**: Rational joint problem solving: separate people from problem, focus on interests, generate win-win options, use objective standards.
- **BATNA**: Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement; still valid preparation, but not enough alone.
- **Cognitive Bias**: Unconscious distortions in how humans see the world; Kahneman and Tversky found 150+.
- **Loss Aversion**: People act harder to avoid a loss than to gain an equal amount.
- **Framing Effect**: The same choice is judged differently depending on its framing.
- **System 1 vs System 2**: Fast, emotional, instinctive thinking steers slow, deliberative thinking.
- **Process fallacy**: Scripts assuming "do a, b, c, d and get x" fail because real negotiation is unpredictable.
- **Negotiation as "I want"**: Most interactions reduce to one party expressing a want to another.
- **The hostage negotiator must win**: Split-the-difference is not an option, yet the relationship must survive.

## Mental Models
- Use emotions as the means, not as obstacles to be overcome.
- Use open-ended questions when the other side bulldozes you; they shift the frame from your problem to theirs.
- Use listening as the cheapest concession you can make: people who feel heard become less defensive and more rational.
- Treat counterparts as "crazy, irrational, impulsive, emotionally driven animals" (as you are), then plan for that.
- Use preparation (the Negotiation One Sheet) rather than a fixed script.

## Anti-patterns
- Assuming the other side is a rational maximizer and arguing with airtight logic.
- Following a fixed sequence of offers and counteroffers like a robot.
- Keeping a poker face and suppressing emotion.
- Treating listening as passive.
- Avoiding negotiation because it feels confrontational; "you get what you ask for, you just have to ask correctly."

## Worked Example
Harvard mock kidnapping: Mnookin plays the kidnapper: "Give us one million or he dies."
- Voss, smiling: "How am I supposed to do that?"
- Mnookin pushes: "So you are okay with me killing your son?"
- Voss, with apology and first name for warmth: "I'm sorry, Robert, how do I know he's even alive? How can I get you any money if I don't know he's alive?"
- Repeating those questions, the kidnapper ends up solving logistics for Voss; Blum takes over and also stalls. In class, Voss repeats three or four open questions and his partner Andy concedes every dime.

## Key Takeaways
- Emotions drive decisions; engage them rather than suppress them.
- Tactical Empathy is active, not passive.
- Repeated open-ended questions make people negotiate against themselves.
- Negotiation is everyday communication with results.
- Prepare with a flexible One Sheet instead of a rigid script.
- Real negotiation tools must work when they cost something; FBI methods were forged where failure meant death.
